Some operators affect the $h$ interactions. For example, we have 
$$
	O_{H} = \frac{1}{2}[\partial_{\mu}H^{\dagger}H]^{2},
$$
which modifies the coupling of a tree-level interaction by 
$$
	g_{HXX}^{SM} \to g^{SM}_{HXX}\left( 1- \frac{c_{H}v^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}} \right).
$$
Because of this, branching ratios stay the same.

From measurements, I can put bounds on operators, for example 
$$
	\Delta S = \frac{g^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\log\left( \frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m_{h}^{2}} \right) \frac{c_{H}v^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}
$$

# Effective theory for Higgs
We can use the effective Lagrangian 
$$
	\mathcal{L}_{eff} = m_{Z}^{2} \frac{h}{v} Z_{\mu}Z_{\mu} + m_{W}^{2} \frac{h}{v} W_{\mu}^{+}W_{\mu}^{-} + m_{\psi}\bar{\psi}\psi \left( 1 + \frac{h}{v}c_{f} + \frac{h^{2}}{v^{2}}c_{f}^{(2)} + \dots \right) + F_{\mu \nu}F^{\mu \nu} \frac{h}{v} c_{\gamma\gamma} + G_{\mu \nu}^{a}G_{\mu \nu}^{a} \frac{h}{v} c_{gg}.
$$
I can also use the BSM operators, such as 
$$
	O_{W} = D_{\mu}W_{\mu \nu}(H^{\dagger}D_{\nu}H) \sim \partial^{2}h v W_{\mu}W_{\mu}, c_{W} \sim \frac{\cos\theta_W}{\Lambda^{2}}v \frac{m_{h}^{2}}{m_{W}^{2}}+\dots .
$$
Based on experiments, I get bounds on new physics operators: 
$$
	\frac{\delta g}{g} \leq 1\% \sim c_{i } \frac{v^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}} \implies \Lambda \geq  10v\sqrt{ c_{i} }.
$$
Today, our bounds say $\Lambda \geq \mathrm{few\ Tev}$.

### Lepton colliders
Suppose I want to find the corrections to a certain coupling. From the production of bosons, I have to measure $\sigma(pp\to h)\mathrm{BR}(h\to XX)$, which is difficult ton control.

Using leptons, I can use Higgs strahlung, 
$$
	\sigma(e \bar{e}\to Z h) \sim \frac{1}{s}(g_{V}^{2} + g_{A}^{2})g_{hZ Z}^{2},
$$
and the branching ratios are controlled much better.

In proton colliders, what I know in a collision is that $(p_{h})(T)=0$, the transverse momentum is null.
With leptons, I also know that $P_{tot} = 0$.
 From this, I know that $p_{h}^{2} = p_{Z}^{2}$.

In LHC, I have that 
$$
	\sigma(pp\to h) = \sigma_{SM} g_{ggh}^{2},\quad \mathrm{BR}(h\to WW) = \mathrm{BR}_{SM} f(g^{i}_{H}), \quad\sigma_{SM} \cdot \mathrm{BR}_{SM} = g_{ggh}^{2} f(g^{i}_{h}).
$$
