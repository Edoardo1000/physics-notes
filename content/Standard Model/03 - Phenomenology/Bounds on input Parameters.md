
### Experimental Bound on the W Boson Mass
From the tree-level relations derived in [[Gauge Boson Mixing and Neutral Currents]], we can express the mass of the $W$ boson as:
$$m_{W}^{2} = \frac{\pi \alpha_{\mathrm{em}}}{\sqrt{ 2 }G_{F}\sin ^{2} \theta_{W}} = \frac{(37.3 \ \mathrm{GeV})^{2}}{\sin ^{2} \theta_{W}}$$

Because $\sin^{2}\theta_W \le 1$, this relation sets a  lower bound on the mass of a weak vector boson: $m_W > 37.3 \text{ GeV}$. 

### Theoretical Upper Bound on the Higgs Mass (Weak Coupling / Triviality)
From [[Higgs Boson Interactions]], the Higgs mass is governed by the quartic coupling $m_{h}^{2} = 2\lambda v^{2}$. 

If we demand that the Standard Model remains a weakly coupled, perturbative theory at high energies (meaning 2-loop corrections are smaller than 1-loop ones), we must limit the size of $\lambda$. 

> [!note] A Note on the Loop Bound Math
> If we impose a strict cutoff where the theory becomes strongly coupled ($\lambda < 16\pi^2$), the math yields $m_h^2 < 32\pi^2 v^2 \sim 4.4 \text{ TeV}$. If we apply a more realistic tree-level partial-wave unitarity bound ($\lambda < 2\pi^2$ or $\lambda < 4\pi$), we arrive at the classic electroweak upper limit:
> $$m_h \lesssim 1.2 \text{ to } 1.4 \ \mathrm{TeV}$$
> If the Higgs were heavier than this, the weak force would have become strong at high energies, completely ruining perturbative QFT calculations.

## Precision Observables as BSM Windows
Because the input schemes fix our parameters so precisely, any slight deviation between a derived parameter and its actual measured value indicates **Beyond the Standard Model (BSM)** particles running in loops.

* **Example:** The anomalous magnetic moment of the muon, $(g-2)_\mu$, is measured to such extreme precision that discrepancies between the Standard Model calculation and experimental data serve as a premier hunting ground for supersymmetry, dark photons, or extra Higgs doublets.
