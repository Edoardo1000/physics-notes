This theory is a **UV-completion** of the [[Composite Higgs#Minimal composite Higgs|minimal composite Higgs]] model. The minimal composite Higgs is the resulting *sigma model* after taking the *decoupling  limit* of this theory.

This theory supposes that Higgs is the composite state of **technifermions** $\psi$, in a representation of $SU(N)$ with its relative gauge field. The theory has also its cutoff scale 
$$
	\Lambda_{TC} \approx 4\pi f \geq \mathrm{TeV}.
$$
The technifermions form a **condensate**
$$
	\langle \bar{\psi}\psi \rangle \sim \Lambda_{TC}^{3}.
$$
In this theory, $h$ is a **resonance**, along with *other possible resonances* $\rho_{i}$.

In  this theory, because of the resonances the correlation functions of the vector and axial current, $\Pi_{V}(q^{2}), \Pi_{A(q^{2})},$ get various corrective terms. In particular, we have
$$
	\begin{align}
	\Pi_{V}(q^{2})  &  = \sum_{n} \frac{f_{n}}{q^{2}-m_{f_{n}}^{2}}(q^{2} g_{\mu \nu}-q_{\mu}q_{\nu}), \\
	\Pi_{A}(q^{2}) & = \sum_{n}\left( \frac{f_{n}^{(a)}}{q^{2}-m_{f_{n}}^{2}}  - \frac{f_{\pi}}{q^{2}} \right)(q^{2}g_{\mu \nu}-q_{\mu}q_{\nu}).
	\end{align}
$$
As a consequence, the $S$ parameter becomes
$$
	S \approx \Pi'(0) \approx \left( \frac{f_{\rho}^{2}}{m_{\rho }^{2}}-\frac{f_{a}^{2}}{m_{a}^{2}} \right),
$$
which gives us a way to probe the theory.

# Interaction with fermions
In order to give mass to Standard Model fermions, I need a **Yukawa-type coupling**  between the fermions and the technifermions.

Thus, I need operators of the form $\bar{\psi}_{L}\psi_{R}\mathcal{O}_{H}$, with $\mathcal{O}_{H} \sim \Psi\bar{i} \Psi$.

So I expect interactions like 
$$
\frac{1}{\Lambda_{UV}^{2}}\bar{q}_{L}q_{R} \bar{\Psi}\Psi,
$$
which  implies
$$
	Y_{q} \sim \left( g_{*} \frac{\Lambda^{2}}{\Lambda_{UV}^{2}} \right)^{[\mathcal{O}_{H}]-1}.
$$
Notice that the *anomalous dimension* of $\mathcal{O}_{H}$ might be large in a strongly coupled regime. Observe that if $[O_{H}] \sim 1$, I lose sensibility to UV effects.

Because of  this and loop effects, I get constraints on $\Lambda_{UV}$, which must be much greater than $v$ since I do not see these effects.
The problem is that, since $\Lambda_{UV}$ is big, $Y_{q}$ risks to become *too small*. The only way to prevent this is to set $[O_{H}]\sim 2$. This, in turn, implies that the Higgs is not really a composite particle.
Thus, **if I want to solve the naturalness problem, the composite Higgs is not the solution**.

#### Alternative coupling
This time I try 
$$
	\lambda \bar{q} \mathcal{O}_{\psi}, \quad \lambda(\mu) = \lambda(\Lambda_{UV}) \left( \frac{\mu}{\Lambda_{UV}} \right)^{\gamma},
$$
this coupling has no analogue in QCD.

**Partial compositeness.**
