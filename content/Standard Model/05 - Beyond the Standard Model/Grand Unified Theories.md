Since the various [[Glashow-Weinberg-Salam Model|quantum numbers]] of the Standard Model are rational, one might try to **extend the Standard Model group into a larger unified one**.
# Georgi-Glashow model
The group is 
$$
	SU(5),
$$
with many generators:
- an **octect** made with $\lambda^a$;
- a **triplet** made with $\sigma^a$;
- a $(3,2)$ **bifundamental representation** (color + $SU(2)$), they are *new bosons* $X_{\mu}^{\alpha i}$;
- $T^{24} = \mathrm{diag}(1/3,\; 1/3,\; 1/3,\; -1/2,\; -1/2)$.
Because of this bosons which we do not see, the theory must live at a very high-energy scale.

## Fermions
We have 
$$
	Y \propto T^{24}.
$$
Fermions should probably belong to the *fundamental representation $\mathbf{5}$*. In fact, we have 
$$
	\mathbf{5} = (3,1)_{1 / 3} + (1,2)_{- 1 / 3}, \quad \psi_{\mathbf{5}} = \{ \ell_{L}, d_{R} \}.
$$
And the other fermions are in a *decuplet* 
$$
	\mathbf{10} = (3,2)_{1 / 6} + (3,1)_{2 / 3} + (1,1,)_{1},\quad \psi_{\mathbf{10}} = \{ q_{L},u_{R}, e_{R} \}.
$$
## Symmetry breaking
We have our scalar field belonging to the *adjoint representation* 
$$
	\Phi \in \mathbf{24},\quad \mathcal{L} \supset \lvert D \Phi \rvert ^{2} - V(\Phi).
$$
# Theory cutoff
Becaue of the bosons $X_{\mu}^{\alpha i}$, the cutoff must be really large. We have 
$$
	\mathbf{24} = (8,1)_{0} + (1,3)_{0} + (1,1)_{0} + (3,2)_{-5 / 6} + (\bar{3},2)_{5 / 6}.
$$
A cutoff can be imposed by looking at **proton decay**, one of the processes predicted by the theory. In fact, we have operators such as 
$$
	X_{\mu}^{*}\bar{q}u^{c},\qquad X_{\mu}^{*} \bar{e}q,\qquad X_{\mu} \bar{\ell} q,
$$
which are **leptoquarks**.
The decays are for example 
$$
	p\to \pi^{0} + \bar{e},\qquad p\to \pi^{+} \bar{\nu},
$$
and I expect 
$$
	\Gamma \sim \frac{m_{p}^{5}}{M_{X}^{4}} \implies M_{X} \geq 10^{15} \ \mathrm{GeV},
$$
which is surprisingly similar to the $10^{14} \ \mathrm{GeV}$ scale of the neutrino mass operator.
# Pati-Salam model
In alternative, one might try to expand the idea of $$SU(2)_{R}.$$
Looking at the table, I might try to *unify quarks and leptons*, leptons become just a new color: 
$$
	\mathbf{4} = \mathbf{3} + \mathbf{1},
$$
with quarks 
$$
	\psi_{L} = \{ q_{L}, \ell_{L} \}\sim(4,2,1),\qquad \psi_{R}=\{ q_{R}, \ell_{R} \} \sim (4,1,2).
$$
Now our symmetry group beomes  
$$
	SU(4) \times SU(2)_{L} \times SU(2)_{R} = SO(6) \times SO(4) = SO(10).
$$
Moreover, I can also break the group another incompatible way: 
$$
	SO(10)\to SU(5) \times U(1)_{B-L}.
$$
The cutoff is at $O(1000 \ \mathrm{TeV})$. 
This time, since $B-L$ is a gauge symmetry, we have **no proton decay**. 

However, we have processes like 
$$
	\bar{K}^{0} \to \mu +  \bar{e}.
$$
In the Standard Model, the amplitude is zero because of the neutrino mass.
