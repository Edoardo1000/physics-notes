## Electroweak Vacuum 
* **Higgs VEV ($v$):** *246.22 GeV* (Derived from the Fermi constant $G_F$)
* **Weinberg Angle ($\sin^2\theta_W$):** *0.23155* ($\overline{\text{MS}}$ scheme at the $Z$ pole)
* **Fine Structure Constant ($\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$):** *1/137 ± 10^-15* (At zero energy, known from atomic physics and $g-2$)

**Note**: The fine structure constant is subject to [[Running of the Electromagnetic Coupling|running]], which greatly decreases its precision at high energies.

## Electroweak Input Schemes
 At tree level, the Electroweak Lagrangian contains a set of intertwined parameters ($g, g', v, \lambda$). To make real-world experimental predictions, we must choose a subset of these as independent **input parameters** based on what we can measure with the highest precision. We then derive everything else from them.

Because loop corrections (radiative corrections) alter the relationships between parameters, our choice of an **input scheme** dictates how cleanly we can calculate observables.
Possible scheme choices:
- **Standard EW Scheme $\{ \alpha_{\mathrm{em}}, G_{F}, m_{Z} \}$:** constants known with the best precision;
- **Alternative Scheme $\{ G_{F}, m_{W}, m_{Z} \}$:** It fixes the masses of both heavy vector bosons directly. It seems less precise due  to $m_{W}$, but it does not have the problem of the running $\alpha$.
