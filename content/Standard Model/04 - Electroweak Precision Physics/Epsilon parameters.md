 We calculated [[Effective EW parameters|effective parameters]] such as $\Delta \rho, \Delta \kappa$, but they are not very revealing in terms of predicting masses.
 There is a set of observables which is often used, this is 
$$
	\begin{align}
	\epsilon_{1}  & = \Delta \rho, \\
	\epsilon_{2}  & = c_{W}^{2} \Delta \rho + \frac{s_{W}^{2}}{c_{W}^{2}} \frac{\Delta r_{W}}{c_{W}^{2}-s_{W}^{2}} - 2s_{W}^{2}\Delta \kappa, \\
	\epsilon_{3} & = c_{W}^{2} \Delta \rho + (c_{W}^{2}-s_{W}^{2})\Delta \kappa.
	\end{align}
$$
For the Standard Model we have 
$$
\begin{align}
	\epsilon_{1}^{SM}  & = \frac{3G_{F}m_{t}^{2}}{8\sqrt{ 2 }\pi^{2}}- \frac{3G_{F}m_{W}^{2}}{4\sqrt{ 2 }\pi^{2}}\tan ^{2}\theta_{W} \log \frac{m_{h}}{m_{t}}, \\
	\epsilon_{2}^{SM}  & = -\frac{G_{F}^{2}m_{W}^{2}}{2\sqrt{ 2 }\pi^{2}}\log \frac{m_{h}}{m_{Z}}, \\
	\epsilon_{3}^{SM}  & = \frac{G_{F}m_{W}^{2}}{12\sqrt{ 2 }\pi^{2}}\log \frac{m_{h}}{m_{Z}} - \frac{G_{F}m_{W}^{2}}{6\sqrt{ 2 }}\log \frac{m_{t}}{m_{Z}}.
\end{align}
$$
With the measurements we had bounds on $m_{t},m_{W}$, assuming $m_{h}=125 \ \mathrm{GeV}$.

The [[Extensions of the Standard Model|heavy new physics parameters]] give corrections to the variables: 
$$
	\epsilon_{i} \to \epsilon_{i}^{SM} + \Delta\epsilon_{i} = \epsilon^{SM}_{i} + \Delta\epsilon_{i}\bigl|_{SD} + \Delta\epsilon_{i}\bigl|_{LD},
$$
where $SD,LD$ stand for short distance and long distance corrections. We have
$$
	\begin{align}
	\Delta\epsilon_{1}\bigr|_{SD}  & = T - W - Y \frac{s_{W}}{c_{W}} + 2X \frac{s_{W}}{c_{W}}, \\
	\Delta\epsilon_{2}\bigr|_{SD}  & = U - W -V + 2X \frac{s_{W}}{c_{W}}, \\
	\Delta\epsilon_{3} \bigr|_{SD}  & = S - W - Y + \frac{1}{s_{W}c_{W}}X.
	\end{align}
$$
In this case, $\Delta\epsilon_{2}\bigr|_{SD}$ is small with respect to the others.
