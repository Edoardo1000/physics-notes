*This note is based on Peskin and Schroeder, An Introduction to Quantum Field Theory, Chapter 11.*

The quantum effective action is a tool to understand how quantum effects affect dynamics observed at the classical level. Moreover, it is the best approach to treat tools in the presence of global symmetries.

For this discussion, we will keep $\hbar$ explicit.

Consider a QFT with a field $\varphi$, with generating function 
$$
	Z[J] = \int \mathcal{D}\varphi \; e^{ -(S[\varphi] + J_{x} \varphi_{x})/\hbar}\equiv e^{ W[J]/\hbar },
$$
where we use the suppressed notation 
$$
	J_{x}\varphi_{x} \equiv \int d^{4}x J(x) \varphi(x).
$$
The expectation value of the field is 
$$
	\langle \varphi(x) \rangle_{J} = \frac{\delta W}{\delta J(x)}\equiv \phi(x).
$$
Now, in the path integral perform the change of variables 
$$
	\varphi = \phi + \sqrt{  \hbar}\eta,\quad \langle \eta \rangle = 0
$$
and expand 
$$
	\begin{align}
	S[\varphi]  & = S[\phi] + \sqrt{ \hbar } S^{(1)}_{x}[\phi] \eta_{x} + \frac{1}{2} \hbar S^{(2)}_{xy} \eta_{x} \eta_{y} + \dots, \\
	J  & = J_{0} + \hbar J_{1} + \hbar^{2} J_{2} + \dots
	\end{align}
$$
where $S^{(1)}_{x} = \frac{\delta S}{\delta \varphi(x)}$ and so on. Substituting in the path integral, we find
$$
	Z[J] \propto e^{ -(S[\phi]+J_{x}\phi_{x})/\hbar } \int \mathcal{D}\eta \; e^{ -(S^{(1)}_{x} - J_{0,x}) \eta_{x}/\sqrt{ \hbar } - S^{(2)}_{xy}\eta_{x}\eta_{y}/2 + \dots }.
$$
From this, we see that for the condition $\langle \eta \rangle=0$ to be satisfied at the quantum level, the various $J_{i}$ have to satisfy some constraints, for example 
$$
	J_{0,x} = S^{(1)}_{x}[\phi],\quad J_{1,x} = \frac{1}{2} S^{(3)}_{xyz}[\phi]G_{yz},\quad G_{yz} \equiv (S^{(2)}[\phi])^{-1}_{yz}.
$$
The interpretation of the second equation is that quantum corrections to $\eta$ generated by tadpoles are taken into account by the higher order terms of the current.

In analogy with the construction of the Gibbs free energy, let's define the Legendre transform 
$$
	\Gamma[\phi] = -W[J]-J_{x}\phi_{x},
$$
and we have the relation 
$$
	J(x) = \frac{\delta\Gamma}{\delta \phi(x)}.
$$
At one-loop level, we have 
$$
	\Gamma[\phi] \simeq S[\phi] + \frac{\hbar}{2}\mathrm{Tr}\log S^{(2)}.
$$
From the relation with $J$, we see that stationary point of the potential correspond to $J=0$. Thus, we conclude that **the expectation value of fields corresponds to minima of the quantum effective action**.
# Function expansion and 1PI diagrams

If we expand 
$$
	\Gamma[\phi] = \sum_{n\geq_{0}} \frac{1}{n!} \int dx_{1}\dots dx_{n} \Gamma^{(n)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\phi(x_{1})\dots\phi(x_{n}),
$$
then the functions $\Gamma^{(n)}$ are the 1PI vertex functions, for example $\Gamma^{(2)}$ is the inverse propagator.

# The Coleman-Weinberg potential

For a slowly varying scalar field, we can expand the action as 
$$
	\Gamma[\phi] = \int d^{4}x \left[ V_{\mathrm{eff}}(\phi) + \frac{1}{2}Z(\phi)(\partial \phi)^{2} + c_{4}(\phi) (\partial \phi)^{4} + \dots \right],
$$
and if the field is constant then we have 
$$
	\Gamma[\phi] = VT \, V_{\mathrm{eff}}(\phi),
$$
where $VT$ is the spacetime volume. $V_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is called the Coleman-Weinberg potential.

For a constant field $\phi$ with Lagrangian $\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{2}(\partial \phi)^{2} - V_{0}(\phi)$ we have
$$
	S^{(2)}[\phi] = -\partial^{2} + M^{2}(\phi),\quad M^{2}(\phi)\equiv V_{0}''(\phi),
$$
and the Coleman-Weinberg potential at one-loop is given by 
$$
	V_{\mathrm{eff}}(\phi) \simeq V_{0}(\phi) + \frac{1}{2} \int \frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\; \log[q^{2} + M^{2}(\phi)].
$$
The logarithm sums all loop diagrams. To see this, expand 
$$
	\log[q^{2}+ M^{2}(\phi)]=\log[q^{2}+m^{2}]+\log\left[ 1 + \frac{\Delta M^{2}(\phi)}{q^{2}+m^{2}} \right],
$$
where $m^{2}$ is the mass of the field, and $\Delta M^{2}(\phi) = M^{2}(\phi) - m^{2}$. Thus, after removing the first term which is background-independent, we have 
$$
	V_{\mathrm{eff}}(\phi) - V_{0}(\phi) \simeq \frac{1}{2} \int \frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\; \left[ \frac{\Delta M^{2}(\phi)}{q^{2}+m^{2}} - \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{\Delta M^{2}(\phi)}{q^{2}+m^{2}} \right)^{2}+ \dots \right],
$$
and we notice that the single terms are loops with $\phi$ insertions, divided by their symmetry factors.
# Bosons and fermions

For bosons, we have 
$$
	\int \mathcal{D}\phi \; e^{ -\eta A\eta/2 } = \left( \det \frac{A}{2\pi} \right)^{-1/2},
$$
for fermions the identity is 
$$
	\int \mathcal{D}\psi \bar{\psi} \; e^{ -\bar{\psi} D \psi }= \det D.
$$
Because of this, we see that fermion contributions to the quantum effective action have **opposite sign** with respect to bosons.
