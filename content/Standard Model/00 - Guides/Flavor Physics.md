Flavor physics studies how quarks of different generations mix, how this mixing appears in weak interactions, and how it produces observable decay rates, oscillations and CP violation.

## Foundations

- [[Global Symmetries of the Standard Model]] - Flavor symmetry belongs to the broader class of global symmetries in the Standard Model (even though it is explicitly broken).

The key idea is that quark [[Electroweak Mass Generation#Fermion Mass Generation|Yukawa matrices]] are diagonalized using different matrices, and this mismatch appears in the charged-current interaction as the CKM matrix

- [[CKM Parametrization and Geometry]]

## Decays and electroweak effective theory

The CKM matrix introduces flavor-changing interactions, and thus new types of decays. Nonleptonic decays are in general more difficult to treat because of the more complicated renormalization structure.

The various operators can be also organized in an electroweak effective theory, basically a generalized version of the original [[Fermi Interaction and Neutron Decay|Fermi interaction]].

## Mixing and CP violation

One of the parameters of the CKM matrix, its phase, determines CP violation in the electroweak sector.

Not all of its values are physical, and we have to identify physical observables:
- [[Flavor Measurements and CP Violation]].

The physical processes which enable us to measure the CKM matrix parameters belong to three broad categories:
- [[Meson Mixing]];
- [[Direct CP Violation]];
