In nature, each fermion comes in three possible *flavors*, not just one. The *kinetic part* of the Standard Model is symmetric under mixing of particles within a given type. 
From this, we deduce that there is a very large symmetry group, and the transformation is called **flavor symmetry**:
$$
	G_{f} = SU(3)^{5} 
$$
under which a fermion transforms as 
$$
	\psi \to U\psi, \quad U \in SU(3).
$$
## Breaking of flavor symmetry 
The [[Electroweak Mass Generation#Fermion Mass Generation|Yukawa sector]] *explicitly* breaks this symmetry. If we look at the Yukawa interaction with more than one flavor, $Y_{u},Y_{d},Y_{e}$ can in fact be general **matrices** which mix different flavors.

Under flavor symmetry, the new coupling corresponds to transforming the Yukawa matrix by
$$
	Y_{f} \to U^{\dagger}_{f_{L}} Y_{f} U_{f_{R}},
$$
with  $U_{f_{L}}, U_{f_{R}}$ different special unitary matrices. These matrices are thus not invariant under the symmetry group.
