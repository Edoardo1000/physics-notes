*The field and their charges are found in [[Glashow-Weinberg-Salam Model]].*

The group of transformations is given by $$
	 \frac{U(1)^{5}}{U(1)_{Y}} =  U(1)_{B} \times U(1)_{L} \times U(1)_{B_{A}} \times U(1)_{L_{A}}.
$$
#### Motivation
I can rotate each field of the Standard model by a phase factor. So there are five possible independent rotations, but we already know one, which is hypercharge. The other nontrivial ones are called Baryon and Lepton number symmetries (note that the right fields $u,d,e$ have been conjugated, so that they are now left):
- **Baryon number:**
 $$
  	q\to e^{ i\alpha/3 }q, \quad u \to e^{ -i\alpha/3 }u, \quad d \to e^{ -i \alpha/3 }d,
  	$$

- **Lepton number:** 
  $$
  	L\to e^{ i\alpha }L, \quad e\to e^{ i\alpha }e.
  $$

- **Axial baryon number:** 
  $$
  	q \to e^{ i \alpha/3 \gamma_{5} }q, \quad u \to e^{ -i\alpha/3 \gamma_{5} }u, \quad d \to e^{ -i\alpha/3 \gamma_{5} }d.
  $$
  
- **Axial lepton number:** 
  $$
  	L\to e^{ i\alpha \gamma_{5} }L, \quad e \to e^{ -i\alpha \gamma_{5} }e
  $$


Not all of this are in fact symmetries. In particular, after the explicit breaking due to the [[Electroweak Mass Generation#Fermion Mass Generation|Yukawa interaction]], only the sum of baryon numbers and the individual lepton numbers are conserved, and only the vector ones. Thus, the symmetry group is 
$$
	U(1)_{B} + U(1)_{e} + U(1)_{\mu} + U(1)_{\tau}.
$$

In addition, it turns out that only the combinations $\frac{B}{3}-L_{e,\mu,\tau}$ are not [[Anomalies in the Standard Model|anomalous]].
