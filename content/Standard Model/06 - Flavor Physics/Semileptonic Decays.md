Semileptonic decays are usually **cleaner** than nonleptonic processes. This is because **QCD corrections** do not affect the leptonic current.

Examples of processes are 
$$
	K \to \pi + \ell + \bar{\nu},\quad B \to X_{c} + \ell + \bar{\nu}
$$

# Effective Lagrangian
The interaction term is given by 
$$
	\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{semilep}} = \frac{4G_{F}}{\sqrt{ 2 }}V_{ij} (\bar{u}_{L}^{i}\gamma_{\mu}d_{L}^{j}) (\bar{\ell}_{L} \gamma^{\mu}\nu_{L}).
$$
Suppose we are in the **massless quark limit**. In this case, among the possible operators which are generated by *loop corrections*, the ones mixing left and right fermions cannot appear due to chiral symmetry. Moreover, since we started from only left fermions, right ones cannot be generated from gluons. Thus, the only possible operator is of the form 
$$
	\bar{q}_{L}\gamma_{\mu}q_{L},
$$
which is the *conserved left-chiral current*. Because of this, **the current does not renormalize**.
Thus, **the one-loop effective Lagrangian does not run**.

The only problem is that $\langle \bar{u}^{i}_{L} \gamma_{\mu} d_{L}^{j} \rangle$ *is nonperturbative*, which means it must be extracted from experiments or lattice QCD calculations.
